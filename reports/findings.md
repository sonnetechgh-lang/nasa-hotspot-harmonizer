# Ghana Fire Calendar — key findings

_Generated 2026-11-05 from data up to 2026-11-04. Units: VIIRS-equivalent active cell-days (a 0.025° cell with fire on a day)._

## The harmonization problem and result

- Raw hotspots: VIIRS records about **3.9×** as many as MODIS over Ghana. Joined naively, burning appears to jump in 2012.
- On a common 0.025° grid the gap shrinks to **2.85×**; the rest is small fires only VIIRS can see.
- Monthly correction factors (fitted 2012–2025) predict held-out years' VIIRS totals from MODIS with **3.3%** average error (10% for single fire-season months).
- Factor ranges from 2.3 (main season, big fires) to 12.3 (off-season, small fires).
- Before 2002-07-19 only Terra was flying; a separate Terra-only factor is used (7.3% error) and those months are marked lower confidence.

## Ghana as a whole

- Usual fire season: **27 Nov – 15 Mar**, peak around **4 Jan**; half of all burning between 13 Dec and 2 Feb.
- Burning in 2021–25 averaged **−28%** compared with 2003–11.
- Last full season (2025/26): **−38%** vs normal. Lowest season on record: 2025/26 (−38%); highest: 2006/07 (+25%).
- Last 30 days (2026-10-06 to 2026-11-04): normal (1,096 vs 980).

## Regions

- **The season moves south.** Upper East peaks first (around 23 Nov), Central last (around 18 Apr): northern savanna burns in November–December, the middle belt in January, the southern forest zone in March–April.
- **Where the decline came from.** Savannah, Northern, Bono East and Bono account for **68%** of the national drop.
- **Steepest relative drops:** Bono (−59%), Western (−55%), Central (−42%).
- **Little or no decline:** Western North (+8%), Greater Accra (+1%).

## Caveats

- Hotspots measure burning activity seen by satellites, not the number of fires or burned area. Fires between overpasses or under thick cloud are missed.
- Off-season months before 2012 multiply small MODIS counts by large factors and are less certain (under 5% of a year's burning).
- The long-term change carries the calibration's uncertainty (a few percent a year), much smaller than the −28% change.
- The latest weeks use near-real-time data, replaced by quality-checked data a few months later.
